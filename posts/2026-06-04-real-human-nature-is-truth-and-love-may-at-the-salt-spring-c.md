## *A TEACHING ON SATYA *

May is a generous time at the Centre - the land full, the days long, and a quiet sense of something being asked of us. This month we are sitting with Satya, truthfulness, the second of Patanjali’s Yamas in Chapter 2 of the Yoga Sutras. It follows Ahimsa, non-harming, and the sequence matters: truth without compassion is not yet fully Satya.

Babaji went to the root of it:

*"Real human nature is truth and love."

~ Baba Hari Dass *

This is not a description of what we should become. It is what is already there, beneath the layers of story, defence, and performance. Babaji described it elsewhere as a gold piece covered in mud: the covering does not change what the gold is. The practice is the clearing.

"A gold piece covered with dried mud looks like a rock, but as soon as the mud covering is broken the gold piece shines and is clearly separate from the mud. This body, the mind, the senses, and their creation of worldly illusions are like a covering on the Self. They hide its glory, but the Self is never really affected by them."

— Baba Hari Dass

The mud is not the gold. The practice is the clearing. Patanjali names the fruit of that clearing in Chapter 2, Sutra 36: When one is established in truthfulness, actions and their fruits come into natural correspondence. Not a reward rather a structural result. When the gap between what we know and what we do closes, action and outcome align.

The Bhagavad Gita approaches the same place through dharma. Chapter 3 names svadharma "one’s own rightful path" as more worthy than another’s followed perfectly: to uphold dharma is to stop performing someone else’s version of who we should be. Babaji named what blocks this simply: the ego keeps the door shut. Satya is one of the keys, not because it is noble, but because it is accurate. If this theme is calling to you, our Yoga & Wellness Weekend Retreats this June and July offer the space to sit with it — in practice, in stillness, and on the land.

## Walking with one Another

This month we were humbled to welcome 90 members of the Q’ushin’tul’ — “walking with one another” in Hul’q’umi’num’ — to rest on our land beside the Pond Dome. Led by Quw’utsun Elders, their four-day journey across Salt Spring Island carries the teachings of Peace, Unity, Family, Healing, and Belonging. May also brought first harvests from the farm, lilac syrup, a weekly flatbread quest in the kitchen, and the closing week of our spring cohort.
