The story

A building that refused
to be demolished

The timbers beneath the Centre School have journeyed farther than most buildings ever will — from an ancient forest, to a booming logging town, to the heart of Salt Spring Island.

1940s
Kokish, North Vancouver Island
Ancient old-growth firs selected

Crown Zellerbach established a logging town of 500 people near Telegraph Cove in the booming era of BC forestry. The families moving in needed a school — and unique old-growth Douglas Fir trees were specially selected for the building. The same trees that anchor BC’s coastlines with deep roots became its bones. The perfect structure for a school, literally and figuratively.

1982
Bonanza Valley closure
A demolition ad, a bold bid

By 1980 the magnificent forests of Fir and Cedar were decimated. The town closed in 1982. A demolition notice appeared in the local paper for the buildings — including the school. Satsang brother Chakrapani (Roland Emery) saw the ad, placed a bid, and won. Fellow Satsang members Sanatan, Maheshwar and Sri Nivas went up to take it down and bring it back. Two massive flatbed trucks were loaded and hauled south to the newly established Salt Spring Centre of Yoga property on Blackburn Road.

Now
Salt Spring Centre of Yoga, Blackburn Road
Thriving again — and growing fast

The school ran for 42 years until it was closed due to lack of enrollment. But it is home to school children again. The Nature Education Academy has 24 children in their first year, and 40+ children registered for next season. StageCoach Theatre classes are running all summer. The annual Kids Program at the Summer Retreat is back. And adult agricultural community classes — medicinals, orchard, seeds, herbology — are beginning later this year. The legacy is very much alive — but the roof cannot wait.
